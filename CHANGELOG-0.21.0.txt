OOTP Front Office 0.21.0 — everything since 0.20.0

New — The assistants can see the trading block. OOTP records which players a club has actually listed, the export has carried it all along, and nothing here read it — so every assistant reasoned about the league as though all thirty clubs were equally willing to deal. They are not: in the save this was built against, 153 men are listed across 25 clubs, and the four best clubs in the league have listed nobody at all. The staff chat can now ask for the block, by club or across the league. The trade desk is told which men in the deal in front of it are listed, and what that means — a club that has listed a player wants to move him and the price starts lower, while a club that has not is being asked for a favour. The weekly briefing carries the best dozen. Asked about Tampa Bay before this, the desk named two men it "would not touch" who were both on Tampa's block, and picked out a target in San Diego who was not on the market at all, while ten Tampa players it never mentioned were up for sale.

Fixed — A roster line is now what the man did on that roster. A reader watched the trade desk put an opposing outfielder forward as "the best position-player season on that roster — .313/.372/.552, 155 wRC+" and asked whether those were Triple-A numbers. They were. He had shuttled between levels, and his line at each was being added together and served as one major-league season. The scaling hid it rather than exposing it: wRC+ and OPS+ are worked out against the club's own league, so a Triple-A line arriving on a major-league roster was measured against major-league pitching and came out extraordinary. This fed the Rosters page, the staff chat and the trade desk alike. It was not rare — one man in this save was reading .241 on a major-league roster while hitting .000 in his six major-league trips.

Fixed — A prospect is judged on the level he is playing at. Another reader was told his Triple-A outfielder was "a statistical outlier at Tacoma, posting a 1.120 OPS and 21 HR in just 181 PAs" and should be considered for a call-up. Every one of the 21 was struck at Double-A; at Triple-A he was hitting a flat .200. The same summed line was then compared against the Triple-A average, so a Double-A season was being measured against Triple-A pitching and called exceptional — the error counted twice in his favour. Promotion signals, the level averages they are judged against, and the minimum sample all now read the level the man is actually at, so somebody who has just moved up has to earn the case again there. Storylines read the same figures and are covered by the same change.
