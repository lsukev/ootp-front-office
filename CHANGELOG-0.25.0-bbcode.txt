[*][b]New[/b] — Every column on Player Search sorts. Click a header to order by it, click again to reverse, a third time to put the table back. The first click takes the direction you meant — highest first for a statistic, A to Z for a name — because asking for the home-run column and being shown the men with none of them is not what anybody wants.

It sorts the whole search, not the page you are looking at. That distinction is the reason this took some care: a typical search matches a few hundred players and a hundred come back, so ordering what arrived would have sorted the page rather than the league, and the man leading a category could have sat on page three and never reached the top of a table sorted by him. The plain columns are ordered by the database; the rate stats are worked out from a league baseline and a park factor and so cannot be, and those searches now compute every match before choosing the page. On the widest search a save can produce — four thousand players across every level — that costs about a sixth of a second.

Men with no line at the level being searched always sort to the bottom, whichever direction you pick, so nobody heads a table sorted by earned run average on the strength of having never pitched.

One thing worth knowing: sorting by a rate will put small samples at the top, because a hundred plate appearances really can produce a better figure than a full season. The Min PA box beside the filters is there for exactly that.
