[b][size=4]OOTP Front Office 0.11.0 — everything since 0.9.6[/size][/b]
[list]
[*][b]New[/b] — Choose your AI service. Anthropic, OpenAI or Google Gemini, with a model picker for whichever you pick. Each service keeps its own key and remembers its own model, so looking at another and coming back costs you nothing. Anthropic stays the default and nothing about it changes; the other two run the same features on a key you may already be paying for. Everything was tested against the real API on all three rather than reasoned about — storylines, the briefing, trade verdicts and the staff chat with its tools.
[*][b]New[/b] — Storylines and the briefing write in the background. Start one and go and do something else. They used to hold the page hostage until they finished, and leaving lost the wait rather than the work; now the work carries on without you and is waiting when you come back.
[*][b]New[/b] — Generate both automatically after an import. A switch in Settings, off unless you ask for it, and it does nothing until a key is entered. Each generation costs money on your own key and nothing should spend that without being asked.
[*][b]New[/b] — Answer back to the trade desk. The verdict is no longer the end of the conversation: there is a reply box under it, and the deal and everything said about it so far go with each follow-up. Ask what he would want instead, or what changes if you keep a man out of it.
[*][b]New[/b] — The trade desk is your general manager, by name. For the Yankees that is Brian Cashman — 58, 28 years in the job, signed four more years, takes the athlete over the slugger, on-base first, trusts what a man has done over what a scout thinks he might become. All of it read from his coach record. In a save where you hold that chair yourself it steps down to the assistant GM, because the alternative is the app arguing with you in your own name.
[*][b]New[/b] — Type @ in the Room and it offers the names. Pick one and the question is aimed at him; he answers alone instead of all four saying they are not Hal.
[*][b]New[/b] — Overall and potential in fives, if you prefer. Scouting talks in fives — a man is a 55 or a 60, not a 57 — and a switch in Settings will write them that way. Display only: sorting and every calculation keep the exact grade.
[*][b]New[/b] — The trade offers actually sitting in your OOTP inbox, listed in the Trade Center with a Review button that loads both sides into the analyser. Which players go which way is not stored in the message and is worked out from who each man plays for now.
[*][b]Better[/b] — The trade verdict reasons about your roster instead of trading ratings. It says what each man is, reads his line against the level it was produced at, names who he would displace on the 26-man, and weighs it against what the club is actually short of. Value bought where you are already deep is worth less than the number says.
[*][b]Fixed[/b] — Overall and potential now match what OOTP prints. The app had been reading the rounded-to-fives column, which disagreed with the game for more than three quarters of players — Rodón showed 55 against OOTP's 57. The depth chart and farm pages were separately averaging component ratings into their own numbers; they use OOTP's grades now, so one Overall appears everywhere.
[*][b]Fixed[/b] — Storylines. Three faults in a row: a schema the API would not accept, a request the model read as inventing news about real people, and an empty set cached as though it were a result. It is now told plainly that it is writing about a simulated season inside your save, and when something does go wrong it reports what actually came back instead of guessing.
[*][b]Fixed[/b] — A healthy player shown as IL-60. The injured-list flags in an export can be left set after a man is activated; the active roster is believed over them now.
[*][b]Fixed[/b] — A designated-for-assignment player described as your starting third baseman. Being DFA'd or on waivers outranks anything else about him.
[*][b]Fixed[/b] — The Room ignored the coaches you had toggled on. It ran whoever was there when the page loaded.
[*][b]Fixed[/b] — Saving a coach's answer to a player's file when the answer only used a surname, or a nickname, or no name at all.
[*][b]Fixed[/b] — A setting that never saved. The switches added most recently were being dropped on their way to disk: the toggle moved, the request succeeded, and the preference was gone at the next launch. The rounding switch had this fault from the day it shipped.
[*][b]Fixed[/b] — Failures now arrive in words. Google returns its errors as JSON inside JSON and it was going straight to the page. Out of credit, rate limited and a rejected key are now three different messages, because they need three different things from you — and being told to wait when you need to add credit wastes your evening.
[*][b]Fixed[/b] — Gemini models that Google lists but will not run. Several of the older ones answer a real request with 404 on a newer key, and nothing in the catalogue says which. The app now falls back to a model that works, tells you it did, offers a button to make that permanent, marks the dead one in the picker, and remembers — so it buys that 404 once rather than before every generation.
[/list]
